# V13 2026-10-19 13:00 CT — minutes residual quantile transfer (research only)

Question distinct from the previous 12:00 zero-mass/CV test: with the *unchanged* 0.5×prior-3 + 0.5×prior-10 minutes baseline, does conditioning 10th/50th/90th residual quantiles on predicted-minutes buckets improve 80% interval scores over pooled residual quantiles? This is a temporal transfer/coverage audit of the existing R13 bucket idea, **not** a new production minutes model.

Train: 2022–23 only, 10,265 appearances. Existing R13 bucket edges fixed before test: <15, 15–25, 25–32, >=32 projected minutes. Evaluate without refitting on 2023–24 (11,168 appearances; 621 games) and 2024–25 (11,055; 629 games). All source features lag by at least one day; same-day histories update only after prediction. Appearance-conditional; no DNP model.

Primary 80% interval score (lower better, nominal alpha=.2):
- 2023–24: pooled 22.805176, bucket 22.269084, improvement +0.536093, game-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [+0.341693,+0.730300]; coverage 80.83% pooled vs 80.26% bucket.
- 2024–25: pooled 23.061360, bucket 22.368467, improvement +0.692893, CI [+0.516260,+0.869138]; coverage 80.56% pooled vs 80.73% bucket.
- Mean width slightly increases: 15.5846→15.7765 (2023–24) and 15.6030→15.8619 (2024–25). The bucket model improves *interval score*, not necessarily sharpness.
- Largest improvements arise in <15 and >=32 projected-minute groups, where pooled residuals are miscalibrated. The lower 10th-quantile pinball did not improve in either season.

This is a positive **retrospective interval-calibration result**, not a betting result, not a comparison to the full R13 Ridge distribution, and not an untouched holdout: 2024–25 has been screened repeatedly.
